# What He Had No Idea He Had Done

*Epilogue*

---

He died on Marajó, and the herd did not.

That is the whole of the ending, and it took three hundred and sixty years to finish saying.

---

He never knew how many there were. He had bought three ships of them in Lisbon with a fortune he had spent three years liquidating, and had lost the ships, and had watched two of them stand off a reef doing arithmetic and then leave, and the animals that got out of those holds swam to whatever coast the current gave them and walked inland into standing water and were never counted by anybody, ever, at all.

He tried. Of course he tried. He was the most exact mind in Europe and he spent a not inconsiderable part of twenty years attempting to establish a figure — asking, riding out, taking accounts from men who had seen a great many and men who had seen a few and men who were lying — and arrived, in the end, at the only honest result his method could produce, which was that the quantity existed and could not be determined.

He wrote it down once, on Marajó, in the only line of mathematics anyone ever found in his hand there, and then did not develop it:

*A great many. Increasing.*

---

He thought of it, when he thought of it at all, as a loss.

That is the part worth saying plainly. He did not consider himself to have introduced anything. He considered himself to have been robbed by two ships' captains and a reef, and to have salvaged, out of the wreck of an enormous and carefully reasoned enterprise, one animal that came back in the evenings for reasons he never established.

The town remembered it his way, and got it slightly wrong in the direction of comedy, and made him into what he remains: *um francês que ficou conhecido pelos búfalos que ele perdeu no Brasil.* A Frenchman known for the buffalo he lost.

Not the theorems. Not the bench, not the correspondence, not the margin. Not the death he engineered, which worked so well that no one has ever thought to look for it. A failure of cargo management, four thousand leagues from anyone who would have understood what else he had been.

He would have found that funny. He found most things funny for a day or so.

---

What nobody in São Luís could have told him — what he had no instrument for, and no vocabulary for, and would have needed another three centuries of somebody else's science to see — is that *he had not lost anything.*

He had **released** something.

The animals that came out of those holds were not from that continent. Nothing on that coast had evolved alongside them: not the grasses, not the reeds, not the fish in the flooded ground, not the birds, not the caiman, not the people. He had taken a species from one side of the world, converted his entire fortune into it because it could not be seized or stolen or counted, and put it into a wet country that had no answer for it — and then it got away from him, which was the only part he ever knew about.

He had been right that they would thrive. That had been the whole basis of the purchase, argued against every man who advised him, and it was the single most correct commercial judgement of his life.

He had simply not understood what the word meant.

---

Three hundred and sixty years later they are still there, and they are Marajó's — on the flags, in the restaurants, under the police, in every photograph anyone has ever taken of the island. They are the reason the place is called what it is called. Ask anyone on that island whether the buffalo belong there and you will get a look, because the question is not sensible: they have been there longer than anyone's grandmother.

They are also, in the strict technical sense that no one uses out loud, an introduced species.

---

And in a different country, on a different river, there is a second case.

Four hippopotamuses were brought to Colombia in the nineteen-eighties for a private zoo belonging to a man who was killed on a rooftop in Medellín in 1993, after which the animals fended for themselves. There are now about two hundred of them in the Magdalena basin. The Environment Ministry expects more than five hundred by 2030. In April of 2026 the government approved a cull of up to eighty, at a cost of some 7.2 billion pesos, to begin in the second half of the year, near Puerto Triunfo and along the river. They eat enormous quantities of vegetation, they change the chemistry of the water, and they are dangerous to manatees, to turtles, and to people.

Nobody calls them heritage.

The animals are innocent in both cases. The men were not consulted about what they were doing, because neither of them thought he was doing it — one was buying a curiosity, and one was buying a fortune he could carry, and both of them lost control of it in an entirely ordinary way, and died.

**The only real difference between a beloved animal and an invasive one is how long ago the mistake was made.**

Escobar's hippos have had forty years. Fermat's buffalo have had three hundred and sixty. Give the hippos three centuries and the Magdalena will put one on a postcard.

---

There is no moral available here that this book is willing to offer, and it has tried.

He was a good man in the ways that were available to him. He faked his own death to get a woman out of a palace and did not once, in twenty years, ask her for anything in return. He taught the infinitely small to children under a tree because there was nowhere left in the Christian world he could say it out loud. He worked out what his neighbours were and never went and knocked. He listened to both sides all the way to the end.

And he put an African animal into South America, in bulk, for money, and then failed to keep track of it, and the consequence outlived every single thing he ever proved.

*A great many. Increasing.*

He was, in the end, exactly right about the only quantity he could never determine — which is a fair description of the whole of his life, and would have irritated him enormously.
