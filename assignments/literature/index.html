The premise

Pierre de Fermat died at Castres on 12 January 1665, two days after signing his last judgment. There is no grave anyone has ever opened.

Twelve years earlier, during the plague at Toulouse, a colleague wrote to a Dutch scholar announcing Fermat's death — and withdrew it in his next letter. Fermat spent twelve years knowing something nobody else in Europe knew: that a death is trivially easy to publish, and that people are eager to believe one.

In 1665 he petitions for a commission to inspect French interests in Grão-Pará and Maranhão — the worst posting in the French imagination. Thirty years on the bench; nobody asks why. He converts a fortune into three ships of water buffalo, because a dead man cannot carry silver past a customs officer. And at Versailles, a woman the king has stopped admiring from a distance leaves in four days instead of the six weeks his arithmetic required.

Five months later a reef takes the ship. The crew, who worked out what the clerk was in month three and have resented pretending ever since, row away. The silver goes down. What comes up out of the flooding hold is the one cargo everyone told him not to buy.

He loses the asset he counted, and is saved by the asset he was mocked for.

Almost none of this is invented

The world is documented. The plot is fiction. Every date below was verified in a single afternoon and can be checked by anyone in the room.

1615Portugal expels the French — and keeps the French name, which the city still carries
1632The Jesuits ban the teaching of indivisibles. The same year, Galileo is summoned to Rome
1641The Dutch take São Luís with nineteen ships. Expelled 1644. Both occupations leave children
1653Fermat catches plague. A colleague announces his death, then retracts it
1654Recife's Kahal Zur Israel — the first synagogue in the Americas — is abandoned as Portugal returns
1661On Pentecost Sunday the colonists of São Luís drag the Jesuits from their cells and ship them to Lisbon
1665Fermat dies at Castres. His son inherits his offices. Vieira is in an Inquisition cell at Coimbra
1666Descartes' body crosses Europe for eight months and arrives in Paris without its head
1670Clément-Samuel publishes his father's papers — including the note about the margin
1890A ship bound for French Guiana wrecks off Cabo Maguari. The buffalo swim ashore and populate Marajó

One invention carries the series: the animal. Everything else is a room that existed and that nobody has set a story in.

Why now, and why here

São Luís is the only Brazilian capital founded by Frenchmen. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — 220 hectares, four thousand buildings. The set exists and is protected in perpetuity.

Brazil and France signed a bilateral audiovisual co-production agreement in 2017, later extended from cinema to all audiovisual modalities. A treaty co-production is a national work in both countries.

Most projects have to be bent into that shape. This one cannot be bent out of it. A French protagonist in French; a Brazilian setting in Portuguese; a city Frenchmen founded and Portugal kept; Tupinambá presence; and a mixed population that exists because of a French and a Dutch occupation. Remove either country and there is no story.

The series ends where the world still is. Brazil holds 238 million cattle and still cannot tell a lender with confidence which of them exist — the problem Fermat notices in a street in 1666, unsolved in 2026.
